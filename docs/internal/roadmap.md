| Fix the determinism and durability audit findings | Medium | The September 2026 audit ([determinism-audit-2026-09.md](determinism-audit-2026-09.md)) found defects in the promises the product is sold on, several of them reproduced with failing tests. | A replica that falls behind at full rate cannot rejoin, and a new history serves before its replicas are live | Medium | Found by bisecting a 6% throughput loss and a doubled p99.9 tail in Exchange Core's dual-replica TCP benchmark (`lan-bench-suite.sh`, `tcp-dual-repl`, 4 clients × window 56, ~1M orders/s): clean through e063af30, regressed from 83d14a32 (genesis journaled at creation). The primary's health samples and logs show what the bench numbers hide: the second replica is evicted for ring backpressure within two seconds of load starting (`replication ring full — evicting replica (would create sequence gap)`, then `evicting slow replica (ring backpressure timeout)`), reconnects, catches up from the journal, is evicted again at the handoff, and loops — 420 evictions in a 60 s run, `melin_replica_catching_up{slot="1"}` set in 63 of 69 samples. The ack policy (`persisted>=1 && in_memory>=2`) stays satisfied by the replica that held, so the run passes while the primary reads its own journal for catch-up against its own writes; that is the tail. Two defects underneath, both older than the commit that exposed them. (1) The replication ring is sized in slots (`--replication-ring-size`, 256, documented as 128 MiB), one batch per slot whatever its size: at the bench's batch sizes it holds ~57K entries, ~57 ms at 1M/s, so a stall of that length evicts a healthy replica, and once evicted the journal catch-up (disk-bound, seconds) can never close a gap the ring refills in tens of milliseconds, so `bridge_catchup_to_live` cannot converge at rate. Fix: size the ring by bytes or entries (fill a slot across consecutive small batches), so the buffering is the documented one; keep the slot inactive until the bridge is within ring reach of the live tail, and evict only a replica that cannot sustain the steady-state rate. Consider backpressure instead of eviction when the evicted replica is one the ack policy counts on. (2) A new history waits for its *first* replica only (`new history: waiting for the first replica before serving clients`, logged next to `ack policy starts degraded`): before 83d14a32 the genesis streamed through the pipeline for ~4 s after that and the second replica was warm and live before clients arrived; now it catches up from the journal and goes live cold a second before full load. Fix: wait until the ack policy's in-memory target is met by caught-up replicas before serving a new history. With the bench suite made to wait for both replicas to be live before loading, the same build measures at baseline (1.03M/s, p99.9 337 µs, no evictions), which confirms (2) as the trigger and (1) as the reason it does not heal. Reproduction: Exchange Core at `chore/melin-main-bump` or later, `TRANSPORTS=tcp-dual-repl WORKLOADS=throughput ./scripts/lan-bench-suite.sh …`; the suite now gates on live replicas and fails a run with evictions, so the regression is visible as a failed run rather than a few percent. The fix lands with a test that evicts a replica under a full ring at batch sizes well below a slot and sees it rejoin and stay. |

| Monotonic sequencer time, derived from the journal | Medium | The application's clock is assembled from pieces that each hold a different invariant, and they have drifted apart. The tick generator clamps each tick only against the previous *tick* (`transport-core/src/tick.rs`), and its last value resets when the reader starts. Event timestamps are stamped at ingress and can arrive out of order (the "rare producer race" `dispatch.rs` documents). `dispatch` advances `Application::tick` through a watermark for event timestamps but calls it *unconditionally* for journaled ticks, so every tick fires `tick` twice and a tick below the watermark moves the application's clock backwards — after an NTP step, after that producer race, or after a failover to a node whose clock is behind. Worse, the watermark is per-process state, not derived from the journal: it starts at 0 in the live matching stage at boot even after replay (`pipeline.rs`), on snapshot restore (`journaled_app.rs`) and in the shadow stage (`shadow.rs`). So a primary restarting after a clock step back calls `tick` for events that a continuously-following replica skips, and a snapshot taken inside such a window restores to a node that makes calls the live one did not — the same journal driving different application state on different nodes, with nothing to detect it (the hash chain covers the journal's bytes, not the application's state). Rare, since it needs a clock regression across a restart, snapshot or failover, but it is exactly the silent divergence the product promises cannot happen; the exchange's `tick`, for one, stamps the clock its rate limiter reads, so it is not idempotent against a rewind. A one-line guard on the tick path fixes the double call and the backwards tick but not the watermark reset, and would read as solved. Direction: **the sequencer assigns time the way it assigns sequence — strictly monotonic by construction.** Every timestamp a primary issues, tick or event, is clamped against one last-issued value seeded from the recovered journal tail at boot and at promotion; journaled timestamps are then strictly increasing on disk, replicas inherit them from the primary's stream, the dispatch watermark stops being state (an assertion, not a variable), and `ApplyCtx::now_ns` becomes monotonic too, which settles whether `apply` may assume increasing timestamps. The design question is the producers other than the reader — startup events, the promotion epoch bump, the DPDK poll thread — which must share or be ordered against that one value. Afterwards restore the strong contract in `Application::tick`'s rustdoc (weakened to "may repeat or go backwards" to match today's behavior). Acceptance: tests for a restart across a clock step back, a snapshot inside a regression window and a failover to a slower clock, each asserting the same `tick` sequence on every path — the counter determinism test below is the natural harness. Changes replayed behavior, so it needs a journal format change: landing it in the release that already moves to format 15 costs no extra migration; after that it carries its own. Plan: [monotonic-sequencer-time-2026-09.md](monotonic-sequencer-time-2026-09.md). |
| Record the application's event-encoding version in the journal | Medium | A snapshot carries `APP_VERSION` and is refused on mismatch, but journal entries carry no application version at all: every replay — restart, replica catch-up, failover — decodes old entries with whatever `AppEvent::decode` the running binary has. An upgrade that changes an event's layout and still decodes the old bytes replays a *different* history with no error; one that fails to decode surfaces as `CorruptEntry` mid-replay or mid-stream, on a replica, long after the deploy. For a product sold on exact replay and a regulatory audit trail this is the worst failure shape, and today only operator discipline prevents it (`docs/journal.md`, "Changing the Application's Encoding"). Options: **(1)** a per-segment version — the application declares an event-encoding version (plus, optionally, the oldest it can still read, so adding an event kind does not force a snapshot migration); every segment header records the version that wrote it (journal format bump); a node refuses to open a segment it cannot read before replaying anything, rotates to a fresh segment when it starts under a new version so no segment mixes two, and the replication handshake compares versions so a replica that cannot read the primary's stream is refused at connect rather than failing mid-stream; **(2)** a version byte per entry — finer-grained but pays per event for what only changes at upgrades; **(3)** documentation only — the status quo. Recommend (1). Decide alongside it whether snapshot `APP_VERSION` and the event version stay separate (they change for different reasons) and whether the snapshot check should accept a readable range too — today it is exact-match, which leaves a snapshot-layout change with no in-place upgrade path short of an external converter or replay from genesis. Breaking for every application and for journal files, so it should land before applications outside this repo accumulate journals; the application guide's upgrade section depends on it. |
| Degraded acks on a halted node | Medium | A primary halted by losing its last replica goes silent instead of answering. Its response stage is one thread that waits in the durability gate on each reply in order. Under `ram`, `disk+ram` or `two-disks`, the first reply in flight when the last replica leaves parks the stage until a replica returns or the operator swaps the policy, and every refusal and query reply queued behind it waits for the whole halt. Clients see only their own read timeout, indistinguishable from a dead network, at exactly the moment operators and traders want positions. Reproduced by the Exchange Core on a primary + one replica with the replica SIGKILLed, on melin 0.15.0, 0.16.0 and `main` at `84d1b0eb`: on an idle node the write is refused with `ReplicaDisconnected`, but `QueryStats` and the request-sequence query hang, while `/healthz` / `/metrics` keep answering. **The query hang also reproduces under `ack-policy disk`**, where the primary's own journal should confirm every reply, so there is a separate, unidentified bug; root-cause it first. The held writes cannot be refused or discarded: they are sequenced, journaled on the primary, applied, and possibly on a replica whose ack was lost, so their outcome is "confirmed later" or "lost in a failover", never "not applied". Pausing ticks while halted (the former recommendation) does not help: at load the state already contains held writes, and in the `disk` case ticks are not the obstacle. Direction: after a grace period in the halt, release a held reply, write or query, once the **primary's own fsync** covers it, terminated by a new `BatchEndDegraded` frame that says only the primary's disk backs it. Under `disk` nothing changes. The policy is never silently weakened, because every weaker reply is marked, and the client decides whether a degraded ack means done or reconcile. Refusals then stop waiting as a consequence, which makes the per-connection release designs considered earlier unnecessary. Refusals always end in a plain `BatchEnd`. Degraded release is an operator switch, on by default; off keeps today's stall. Breaking for `melin-client` by design. The reply-side half of the "in-flight order contract on pipeline halt" item below. First, as a separate fix: the manual-promotion playbook's `ACK-POLICY disk` swap does not lift the halt today. An explicit swap made while no replica is connected will lift it, latched until a replica reconnects, while every policy keeps halting on replica loss. Fencing reaches a partitioned primary only on contact, so the halt is its only guard. Left open in the plan: taking new writes on replica loss under a replica-backed policy, and a raft leader lease as the real split-brain guard. Plan: [halt-degraded-acks-2026-10.md](halt-degraded-acks-2026-10.md). |
| Promoted-node journal catch-up before serving | Medium | The complete fix for the failover data-loss class the shipped peer-tip veto guards against (see `raft_promotion.rs`: a winner refuses to promote while a reachable peer advertises a higher journal tip, and the caught-up peer campaigns to take over), and what the mature systems in this space do (MongoDB catch-up, VSR state transfer): before opening its client listener, a promoted node asks surviving replicas for their journal tails beyond its own tip and applies the diff, so *any* election winner converges to the acked frontier. Strictly stronger than the veto: instead of steering promotion to the caught-up node, it repairs whichever node promotes — the veto/challenger handshake then becomes an optimization rather than the safety mechanism, and the one case the veto can only warn about (a caught-up peer that dies *after* the primary, leaving its higher tip unreachable) shrinks to the window before its tail was pulled. Also heals the `disk+ram` case where a behind winner forces the caught-up replica's journal into a divergence archive. Cost is a new protocol role — a not-yet-promoted replica serving read-only journal tails on its already-bound replication port — with epoch fencing on that path (never serve a stale-epoch suffix) and mid-transfer failure handling; the existing `catch_up_from_journal` and streaming machinery is reusable, as is the shipped `PeerTips` tracking (it names the node to pull from). The `ram`-policy failover test (`crates/core/server-runtime/tests/replicated_failover.rs`) is the regression harness. |

| Owned-buffer io_uring layer (kernel loans become moves) | Medium | The io_uring use-after-free class fixed across the replication sender, the replication receiver, and the client-facing reader (`crates/core/server-runtime/src/uring_teardown.rs` carries the shared teardown policy) had one root cause: buffers are *borrowed* to the kernel through `unsafe` SQE pushes, so the borrow checker's view ends where kernel ownership begins, and the discipline lives in SAFETY comments promising temporal invariants — four of which were wrong. | Document the in-flight order contract on pipeline halt | Low | The persist-before-ack gate (`crates/core/transport-core/src/pipeline.rs:746-750`) guarantees no client is acked for an event that lacks the copies the active ack policy demands (one or two fsynced copies, and/or a second in-memory copy). | Rebase fastcp on upstream smoltcp and prune the fork | Medium | `crates/core/dpdk/Cargo.toml:31` tracks our smoltcp fork on a feature branch (`melin-engine/fastcp`, branch `feat/zc-rx-window-and-index-eviction`), pinned in `Cargo.lock` several commits behind that branch's own head — the fix that stops the cached hardware address outliving its neighbor entry is on the branch but not in our lock. The fork left upstream shortly after smoltcp v0.13 and has not taken a single upstream commit since; every upstream TCP fix lands in our stack only if someone notices it. Two halves, in this order. **(a) Rebase on current upstream** and re-land the additions we still want — the zero-copy RX path and its configurable segment cap, the descriptor-capacity ordering fix, `Interface::forget_tcp_socket` — which are the three the manifest comment already names as upstreaming candidates; getting them upstream rather than carried is the cheapest long-term outcome. **(b) Prune what the fork accumulated on the way**, because most of it was cost-modelled on an axis Melin does not run on. The third pass of the network latency audit (`docs/internal/network-latency-audit-2026-08.md:113-135`) re-rated the DPDK items against a handful of sockets rather than hundreds and struck the per-connection mechanisms outright; the same test applies to the fork's own per-connection work — the cached hardware-address lookup in the dispatch path (which has already cost us one correctness bug and is redundant on a flat LAN with a handful of static peers, alongside the neighbour cache widened to 64 entries in `dad5977c`), the FxHash O(1) socket-lookup index, the idle-socket skip in egress, and the scattered `#[inline]` hints. Keep only what survives measurement on the burst-depth axis. **Gated on the reproducible public benchmark harness** — pruning without a re-runnable measurement can only be argued, not shown, and "removed it and nothing moved" is the entire deliverable. Endpoint is a fork small enough to publish to crates.io, which also restores the `deny.toml` sources policy (`unknown-git = "deny"`, `allow-git = []`) that the git dependency currently violates. |
| Split replication off the DPDK client poll thread | Medium | On the DPDK path the replication driver runs **inline on the client poll loop**: `DpdkReplicationDriver::tick` is invoked from the same `dpdk_transport.rs` iteration that drains client responses to the wire and pulls client order packets off the NIC (`transport.poll()`), and the replication listener deliberately shares the trading queue (`server.rs`: the poll thread "accepts both trading and replication connections off the same queue"). This couples replication and the client hot path in **both directions**. **(a) Clients starve replication:** under heavy client load the replication sender doesn't get enough cycles to drain the ring, it fills, and a healthy replica is evicted. **(b) Replication stalls clients:** no longer by waiting. A join's disk side (probe, snapshot pre-flight, snapshot, segment seed and catch-up reads) runs on the slot's join worker and is handed to the socket a tick at a time, within a per-tick byte budget; the catch-up→live handoff after it is stepped the same way, its journal passes on the same worker and a ring chunk the socket has no room for held in the ring until it has, so neither waits on the disk or on the joining replica; and a joiner that has acked and then takes nothing for the replication liveness timeout is dropped rather than holding its slot (before its first ack it may be installing a snapshot, and is left to it, at no cost to clients). See "A replica's join off the primary's poll thread" and "The catch-up→live handoff, a tick at a time" in `dpdk-replication.md`. What remains is the cost replication adds to every tick (at most a tick's byte budget per slot handed to the stack, the bound the streaming arm already keeps), during which the clients on the queue wait. The kernel-TCP path is unaffected (each replica gets its own handler thread). (b) is therefore **not urgent**; (a) is what keeps this item at its priority. Fix: give replication its own core — a separate smoltcp socket set on a dedicated DPDK queue, or a shared-nothing handoff from the client poll thread — preserving the single-writer invariant on the replication ring (consumer side only). Promote when DPDK dual-replica reconnect latency becomes a diligence concern. Surfaced by the `perf/handoff-deterministic-close` review; promotes the former "Split the DPDK poll thread" optimization item with the replication-blocks-clients direction added. |
| Stream snapshot transfers from disk in chunks | Low | A replica's join reads the primary's snapshot, and the segment seed's prefix, whole into memory before sending the first chunk (`snapshot_transfer_with` in `transport-core`'s catch-up, shared by both transports). On DPDK this now runs on the slot's join worker, off the poll thread, so it no longer costs links or client traffic; what remains is the sender's peak memory (one whole snapshot body per joining replica, on both transports) and the one wait on the disk a stop still has, since stopping joins a join worker that may be mid-read. Read and send in fixed-size chunks instead, keeping the header validation ahead of the begin frame and making sure the bytes sent match the length that frame announces (the snapshot file can be replaced by the next one mid-transfer, so read from one open handle). Recorded by the "A replica's join off the primary's poll thread" work in `dpdk-replication.md`. |

## DPDK Transport Optimization

| # | Optimization | Est. impact | Complexity | Description |
|---|-------------|------------|------------|-------------|
| 1 | Bypass smoltcp on hot path | Significant latency | Very high | For connected+authenticated clients, parse TCP directly from raw Ethernet frames. Eliminates smoltcp's per-packet overhead (neighbor lookup, socket dispatch, congestion window, timer checks). Custom minimal TCP for steady-state data path only. |
| 2 | Unified transport abstraction over io_uring and DPDK | Moderate maintainability, moderate latency | High | The TCP (io_uring) and DPDK code paths have independent replication senders, response writers, and connection handlers with duplicated logic that drifts (e.g. the journal-recreate-on-reconnect bug was fixed in TCP but not DPDK). Investigate a transport trait that captures the common interface (accept, connect, send, recv, poll) so the replication protocol, response stage, and connection state machine can be written once. The challenge is that io_uring is completion-based (submit SQE, reap CQE) while DPDK/smoltcp is poll-based (call poll, check buffers) — the abstraction must not penalize either model. |

## Deferred

Features with limited near-term value. Will revisit when the core product is mature or a specific buyer requires them.

| Feature | Why deferred |
|---------|-------------|
| SPDK journal | Userspace NVMe driver for journal writes. Bypasses kernel block layer entirely. TCP pipelining already hides fsync latency (fsync and no-persist throughput converged at ~8M/s), so the kernel block layer is no longer a bottleneck. High complexity, minimal expected gain. |
| io_uring journal writes | Shipped and then retired with the O_DIRECT sector writer: the journal stage used to submit `WRITE+RWF_DSYNC` async via io_uring, reaping CQEs non-blocking while encoding the next batch, with `IORING_OP_WRITE_FIXED` against registered batch buffers. The buffered writer is the only writer now, and its write path is being split across a dedicated disk thread instead. Revisit an io_uring backend only behind that thread's seam, and only if a trace shows the synchronous `pwrite`/`fdatasync` pair is the binding cost — note io-wq workers are anonymous, which breaks the thread-targeted off-CPU tracing every journal diagnosis here has depended on. |
| Vectored journal writes | Encode each event into its own stack buffer, pass as iovec array to `pwritev2`. Eliminates memcpy-per-event into the batch buffer. Small gain (~1-2µs/batch). |
| Verbatim byte-path journaling | Write the primary's encoded journal bytes directly on both primary and replica, skipping the decode/re-encode step on the replica side and an encode-from-struct pass on the primary. Saves CPU and reduces journal-stage work on the replay-critical path. Attempted once as a replica-only optimization (branch `replica-verbatim-journal`) but created an asymmetry that diverged fsync batching behavior between primary and replica — reverted in favor of a symmetric decode/re-encode on both sides. Revisit only with a plan to apply it on both sides simultaneously, preserving per-fsync size bounds. |
| io_uring RECVSEND_FIXED_BUF | `IORING_RECVSEND_FIXED_BUF` for registered buffer recv/send. Returned EINVAL on kernel 6.8; should work on 6.10+. Also test `IORING_RECVSEND_BUNDLE` for batched recv. Est. 15-25% throughput. |
| Dual-NVMe journal hedging | Two journal threads on separate NVMe drives, response stage gates on the fastest. Cuts tail latency from P(slow) to P(slow)². Free durability redundancy. Low complexity but requires a second NVMe slot. Revisit when journal fsync is the dominant tail contributor. |
| AF_XDP transport | DaMoN '25 found AF_XDP disappoints vs DPDK for small-message request-response workloads. DPDK transport already in progress. Revisit if DPDK proves insufficient. |
| Chain replication | Chain replication (primary → replica A → replica B) reduces primary fan-out. Dual parallel replication (up to 2 replicas) is implemented; chain topology is deferred. |
| TLS | Most exchange deployments use VLAN instead. Only needed for compliance-driven buyers. |
| Hybrid UDP multicast + TCP recovery for event channel | Current event channel is pure TCP. Multicast would reduce latency for co-located subscribers but adds complexity (gap detection, retransmit). Defer until a buyer needs sub-microsecond market data. |
